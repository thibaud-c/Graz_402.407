Uneven digital geographies

Who can contribute to a digital map, and whose places appear?

What does it mean?

The concept of uneven digital geographies describes inequalities in access to digital technologies, participation in producing information, and the representation of places. Having an internet connection does not guarantee that someone's knowledge appears on a map.

Whose knowledge appears?

The accounts below are invented examples, not findings about Graz residents.

A contributor with reliable access

Imagine someone with a smartphone, mobile data, and time to upload photographs of local parks. Their contributions make these places more visible in a digital map.

A contributor facing access barriers

Imagine someone who shares a phone and has limited mobile data. They know the neighbourhood well, but contribute fewer online records. Their experience can be missing from the map.

A place with few online records may still matter to many people. A readable page can improve access to information, but filling gaps in representation also requires attention to who can contribute.
