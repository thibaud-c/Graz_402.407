Knowing a place from somewhere

Situated knowledge asks us to recognise the position and conditions from which knowledge is produced. What we know about a place is shaped partly by our activities, experiences and relationships. Someone meeting a friend, carrying shopping or working nearby may notice different things about the same square.

In digital geographies, this means asking who made a map's records, when and for what purpose. Keeping that context visible helps us assess the claims. It does not mean that every account is equally reliable or that evidence no longer matters.

All three accounts below are invented. Their points illustrate different viewpoints around central Graz. They are not survey results, precise observation sites or evidence about actual conditions.
