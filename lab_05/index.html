Interoperability and control over access

Interoperability means that different systems can exchange and use information. Files, standards, services and the organisations maintaining them form a geodata infrastructure. They let our page combine city records, regional imagery and a surface-height dataset.

Providers still choose what to publish, how to describe it and which operations to allow. A downloadable feature can be inspected and restyled. A WMS supplies the provider's rendered picture. A common format does not guarantee complete coverage, recent observations or agreement about what a place means.

Our geographical question: who controls what we can download, inspect, change or only view? Connect this to the podcast's discussion of opinionated data.

This simple example reads the whole small file. It demonstrates GeoTIFF values, not remote COG streaming. Read our code in scripts/raster.js.

Why two raster libraries? GeoRaster reads the TIFF into values and geographical information. GeoRasterLayer draws those values on our Leaflet map. A library is reusable JavaScript code, loaded with a <script> tag. Both work with ordinary browser JavaScript.
