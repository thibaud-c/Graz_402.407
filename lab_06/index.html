Invited participation

A city invites residents to suggest improvements on a map. Someone has already chosen the question, the answer categories and the area shown. These choices set an agenda: which issues people are invited to discuss. A form can make some suggestions easy to express and leave others difficult to describe.

Participation can also include helping to choose the questions, interpret the answers and decide what happens next. In this exercise you can submit an idea, but there is no planner receiving it. Think about what would need to happen for a suggestion to influence a decision.

Compare: this form asks about small physical changes. Where would someone express a wish for cheaper housing, less policing, or a place to meet after dark? Try the form, then change one choice.

Two ways to choose a location

Map literacy means making sense of a map through its symbols, scale and landmarks. Clicking asks you to connect a place you know with its position on the map. Searching starts with a place name, but you still need to check whether the result matches the place you mean.

Try both methods. Each selects an orange circle on the map; you can click to adjust a search result.
